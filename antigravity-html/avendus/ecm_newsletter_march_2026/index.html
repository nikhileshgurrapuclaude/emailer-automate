We're back with our quarterly update on what moved India’s equity capital markets. 

Q1 CY2026 highlighted that Indian equities faced turbulence from geopolitical tensions which led to FII outflows and rupee weakness. Valuation compression has opened attractive entry points, and despite volatility, we expect ECM activity to be resilient for the next 12-18 months with record filings worth USD 6.3 Bn in March, and landmark REIT-InvIT fundraises.

Precedent shows that India’s equity capital markets have bounced back strongly from crises, underscoring that the long-term growth story remains intact, with recovery expected to be gradual but constructive.

Key highlights:

Domestic liquidity holds firm
Despite heavy FII selling, domestic institutions stepped up, tripling their net inflows in March compared to February supported by SIP inflows of close to INR 30,000-31,000 Cr. This underscores the conviction of retail investors reflecting confidence in India’s growth story.

Equity valuations reset
Indian equities faced pressure from FII outflows, rupee weakness and global shocks, but the compression in NIFTY PE multiples to ~19.9x has created attractive re-entry levels. The RBI’s neutral stance and steady domestic demand provide a supportive backdrop, suggesting that while recovery may be gradual, valuations are now compelling for long-term investors.

Indian market’s ability to navigate crises
The Iran war and crude volatility triggered a spike in VIX and rupee depreciation, but precedent shows Indian markets have consistently bounced back strongly from past crises. This reinforces the narrative that while near-term risks may weigh on sentiment, India’s equity markets remain resilient and aligned with the broader growth trajectory.

REITs & InvITs show resilience amidst volatility
REITs and InvITs showcased resilience in Q1CY26, outperforming broader indices and attracting yield-oriented investors. 2026 has seen multiple marquee deals in space and the future pipeline looks promising with a INR 50,000 Cr worth of fundraising expected this year.
